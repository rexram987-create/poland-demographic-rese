1. General identity and geographic location

Rabka-Zdrój is a spa town in southern Poland, in Lesser Poland Voivodeship, in the Nowy Targ area, between Kraków and Zakopane. It lies in a northern valley of the Gorce Mountains, in an area where local streams connect with the Raba River system. Its mountain setting, cool air and mineral waters shaped its identity as a place of health, children, rest and recovery.

The town is not only a tourist or medical destination. It represents a meeting point between highland Poland, local wooden architecture, spa institutions, family tourism, wartime memory and Jewish history that has largely disappeared from the physical landscape but remains present in research and remembrance.

2. The place name and the meaning of “Zdrój”

The Polish name of the town is Rabka-Zdrój. The part “Rabka” is the historical place name, while “Zdrój” in Polish refers to a medicinal spring or spa, as in other Polish spa-town names.

The name itself tells the story of the place: not only a mountain settlement, but a town whose modern identity was built around mineral water, healing, health, sanatoria, rest and children’s recovery. “Zdrój” is therefore not merely a linguistic decoration, but a central part of the town’s urban and cultural image.

3. Rabka-Zdrój as a spa town

Rabka was known for salt sources and brine waters. From the 19th century it developed as a spa town, especially as a place for children’s treatment. The springs, mountain air and rural-highland atmosphere made it a health and leisure destination.

The space of Rabka-Zdrój includes a spa park, treatment institutions, holiday houses, local wooden architecture and a tradition of long stays for health purposes. The image of a “children’s town” developed around sanatoria, recovery institutions, children’s events and museums connected with childhood and folklore.

Water and salt: a central basis for the spa’s development.
Mountain air: part of the health image of the place.
Sanatoria: a special focus on children’s treatment and recovery.
Tourism: families, leisure, mountain walking and local culture.

4. “Children’s town”, folklore and local culture

Rabka-Zdrój is associated in Poland with children, family holidays, health and folk culture. The town contains institutions and events linked with children, including the connection with the Order of the Smile Museum. It is also connected with highland culture, wooden architecture, folk music, regional dress and access to the Gorce Mountains and local hiking routes.

On the website it is important to show the delicate contrast: on one side, a place of healing, children, scenery and smiles; on the other, a place carrying a heavy memory from the German occupation. This tension makes Rabka-Zdrój an especially important page, because it shows how one place can contain both healing and a historical wound.

5. Short timeline

1254: early mention of the area in connection with salt sources, according to documentary tradition about Rabka’s springs.
19th century: Rabka develops as a modern spa town.
1864: Rabka begins to gain wider recognition as a spa center.
Late 19th and early 20th centuries: treatment institutions, sanatoria and health tourism grow.
1939–1945: German occupation, activity of the Security Police and SD school, and persecution of Jews in the area.
After the war: a return to the image of a spa and children’s town, alongside an ongoing struggle over memory of occupation crimes.

6. The Jewish context of Rabka-Zdrój

The Jewish context of Rabka-Zdrój differs from that of major Jewish cities such as Warsaw, Kraków or Łódź. Here it is less important to speak of a large Jewish metropolis and more important to understand a regional space in which Jews lived, traveled, traded, came for treatment, worked, were caught under German occupation and were affected by wider circles of persecution.

As a spa town, Rabka was also a place visited by patients, families and travelers. Jews in the region lived within a network of towns, villages, local trade and routes through the mountains of Lesser Poland. The Jewish history of the place therefore includes not only local residents but also Jews from the surrounding area who were connected to the town in different periods.

On the website, Rabka-Zdrój should be presented as a chapter in the Jewish history of Lesser Poland: not a large Jewish center, but a place through which we can understand how German occupation entered even spaces of health, leisure and childhood and turned them into spaces of fear, forced labor and murder.

The uniqueness of Rabka-Zdrój is the meeting between a “children’s spa town” and traumatic Jewish memory. This contrast is highly important educationally and museologically.

7. German occupation, the Sipo-SD school and the Holocaust

During the Second World War, a Security Police and SD school operated in Rabka. It was used to train personnel for the Nazi system of repression. Sources about the period connect the town with persecution, violence and executions of Jews in the region.

This chapter gives Rabka-Zdrój a difficult historical significance: a town associated with healing and children also became, during the occupation, a site of training, terror, persecution and murder. It should be presented with care and respect — neither reducing the entire town to horror, nor blurring the crimes committed there.

Persecution: Jews in the area faced humiliation, forced labor, deportations and violence.
German mechanism: the Sipo-SD school marks the presence of repression structures in the town.
Local memory: the need to recognize that even spa towns carried chapters of violence.
Website presentation: combining the beauty of the place with responsibility for historical memory.

8. Memory and heritage preservation

Jewish and wartime memory in Rabka-Zdrój requires balance. On one hand, Rabka is a living spa town with children, tourists, nature and local culture. On the other, the history of occupation requires marking, research and clear explanation.

A strong website presentation could later include a map of memory sites, a section about the Nazi school, references to research sources, a photo gallery of the town and the Gorce Mountains, and an educational box explaining how Nazi authorities used civilian spaces for repression.

It is recommended to add a real or free-to-use image of Rabka-Zdrój later: the spa park, wooden buildings, Gorce Mountain scenery, or a respectful urban view.
